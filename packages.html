What "qualified" actually means

We Don't Send You Meetings. We Send You Decision-Makers, Ready to Buy.

Every lead-gen company says their leads are "qualified." Almost none of them can tell you what that word actually means. Here's exactly what has to be true before a walkthrough ever lands on your calendar, and why each one protects you.

01 Inside your serviceable area

We only reach out inside the zip codes or radius you set during onboarding. You'll never get a walkthrough for a building three counties away that you'd lose money just driving to.

02 Exclusive to you

This lead is never sold or shared with another cleaning company. You're not quoting against three other crews we sent the same walkthrough to. If you're in the room, you're the only cleaning company we put there.

03 A real decision-maker

The contact is the owner, the facility manager, or the office manager, someone who can actually sign a contract. Not a receptionist collecting quotes for someone else's spreadsheet.

04 A real need

The prospect is actively looking for a cleaning service, or open to switching from whoever they have now. You're not walking into a building where the decision was already made six months ago.

05 Walkthrough attended and completed

The meeting actually happened. Someone showed up, walked the space with you, and had the conversation.

06 A good-faith agreement

If a prospect is wasting your time, you flag it as unqualified and we replace it. We're not going to argue with you about whether a walkthrough was worth your afternoon.

Six checks. Every walkthrough. That's the whole standard.

"If it doesn't meet that standard, it doesn't count. We reschedule it or replace it, at no charge."

If the prospect doesn't show up, we reschedule or replace the walkthrough at no charge. If you believe a walkthrough was unqualified, flag it within 48 hours with a short reason and we'll replace it. This isn't a courtesy. It's the only way this model works for either of us.

Every channel out there promises the same thing: more contracts. None of them tell you what it actually costs once you strip away the pitch. So here it is, in plain terms.

Every walkthrough is checked against 6 written criteria before it counts, and gets replaced free if it doesn't meet the bar or the prospect skips it. It's $120 per qualified walkthrough, plus 5% once you actually close the deal. Your side of it: show up, quote, done.

Other lead-gen agencies

"Qualified" means whatever they decide it means that month, with nothing written down and little recourse once you've signed. You're locked into a retainer whether a single appointment shows up or not, and if you cancel early, you're still out what you already paid. They call it "done for you." What that really means is you find out three months in whether any of it worked.

Google Ads

A click isn't a person, and most of them were never a facility manager, never in your service area, and never going to hire anyone that day. Every dollar disappears the second someone clicks, whether or not it turns into anything, and the bill comes whether you win the job or not. On top of that, you're running campaigns, building landing pages, and chasing cold leads that ghost you.

SEO

Six to twelve months of work before you rank for anything, with zero guarantee it ever brings in a real customer. The spend is ongoing, nearly impossible to tie back to an actual signed contract, and it takes constant content and technical upkeep just to defend the ranking you already have.

Cold calls

Mostly hang-ups, and you have no idea who you're actually reaching until after they've already said no. It's cheap on paper but expensive in the hundreds of hours it eats out of your week, with you or someone on your team dialing strangers all day, every day, indefinitely.

Door knocking

Pure luck whether the right person is even in the building when you knock. There's little cash cost, but it eats entire days you'll never get back, and it's physically exhausting, doesn't scale past one person walking one street, with the weather deciding if you're working today.

Nextdoor / Angi

The exact same lead gets sent to you and three or four other cleaning companies at the same time. You're paying for a lead you're now racing to close before someone cheaper or faster gets there first, and whoever calls back in the next ten minutes wins. That's the entire strategy these platforms create.
