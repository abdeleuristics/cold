How the fleet is operated

      

        
15 sends per mailbox per day
A hard ceiling, not an average. Volume grows by adding mailboxes, never by pushing an existing one harder.

        
3 mailboxes per domain
Enough to make a domain worth the setup, few enough that losing one domain removes only 45 sends a day.

        
SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every domain
Authentication is set at purchase, before the first send. An unauthenticated domain is a burnt domain.

        
One identity across the fleet
Every mailbox signs as the same person, so a prospect who replies twice from different sequences meets the same name.

        
Rotation, not repetition
Sends are spread across the fleet and across the day. No prospect is ever contacted by two mailboxes on the same domain.

        
Replies land in one place
All mailboxes forward to a single monitored inbox. The client answers from one screen, not thirty-nine.

Outscraper

        The one tool that gets us these four fields
      

      

        
What it is
A data service sitting on top of the Google Maps dataset. You hand it a category and a geography; it hands back the matching listings as rows.

        
What comes back
Business name, full address, phone, website, category, rating, review count, opening hours — everything the public listing carries.

        
The second pass
Its contacts scraper then visits each business website and collects the addresses published there. That pass is where the decision maker's email comes from.

        
How it is paid
Per record, not per seat. A pull runs in the dashboard or over the API and exports straight to CSV or XLSX.

        
Where it stops
Not every business publishes a person. Some sites carry only an office inbox or a contact form — those rows still ship, and the copy handles them.

        
Why not a database
Bought lists go stale and get resold. A Maps pull is generated the week you send, against businesses that are open today.
